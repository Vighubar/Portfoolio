## Understanding the Problem

I started by conducting contextual interviews with 12 regular users of Tallinn's public transport, observing their pain points in real commuting situations.

Key findings:
- 8/12 users abandoned the official app in favor of Google Maps
- Real-time information was buried 3+ taps deep
- No proactive communication during service disruptions
- Tourist users couldn't understand the zone system

## User Research & Personas

From the research, I developed three primary personas:

- **Kadri** — Daily commuter who needs instant departure times at her stop
- **Marcus** — Tourist navigating an unfamiliar system with language barriers
- **Liis** — Parent planning multi-modal journeys with accessibility needs

## Design Process

The design went through 3 major iterations:

### Iteration 1: Information Architecture
Restructured the app around the three core tasks identified in research, reducing the navigation from 7 tabs to 3 primary actions.

### Iteration 2: Visual Design
Developed a clean, high-contrast interface that meets WCAG AAA standards, using the city's existing brand colors in an accessible palette.

### Iteration 3: Micro-interactions
Added contextual animations for real-time vehicle tracking and disruption alerts, tested with screen reader users to ensure accessibility.

## Testing & Results

Conducted moderated usability tests with 24 participants across all three persona groups:

- Journey planning task time reduced from 45s to 18s average
- System Usability Scale score improved from 52 to 84
- All participants successfully completed disruption-scenario tasks (vs. 30% with current app)

## Reflection

This project reinforced my belief that good UX design starts with genuine empathy. The most impactful design decisions came directly from observing real frustrations, not from aesthetic preferences.
